# How are the airports connected to the network? — transfer edges today; they should snap to road

Analysis of `output/03_network` (generated by `01_airport_connection.py`).

> **✅ IMPLEMENTED.** The recommendation below is now in the engine: airports **snap onto the road**
> (`mmnet/build.py::_snap_airways_to_road`, driven by `profile.yaml` `snaps:` `{Plane→Road @ airports,
> max_dist 2000}`) — the `Road↔Plane` transfer was removed. A rebuild snaps **171 air endpoints** onto road
> nodes (0 air transfer edges; 76 air nodes now shared with road); giant **98.7 %**, 66 components, North
> Slope still connected. The text below is the original analysis that motivated it.

## Current connection — a TRANSFER edge, NOT a snap
Airports connect to the road via **78 air↔road `Transfer` edges**, not by snapping:
- Driven by `profile.yaml` → `transfers: { from_mode: Road, to_mode: Plane, anchor: airports, max_dist: 10000 }`
  (line 156), built in **`mmnet/assemble.py::connect_multimodal` phase 3** (anchor transfers): at each
  airport, the nearest air node is joined to the nearest road node by a new `Transfer` linestring.
- Transfer length: **32 – 6,676 m (median 438 m)**.
- **0 air nodes coincide with a road node** — the air node and the road node stay separate, bridged by a
  fabricated edge. So airports are **not** snapped onto the road (unlike the fuel hubs, which DO snap).

## Snapping is feasible — airports are on/near roads
Distance from each of the 84 airports to the nearest road node:

| ≤ 100 m | ≤ 500 m | ≤ 1 km | ≤ 5 km | ≤ 10 km | ≤ 50 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 54 | **78** | 81 | 81 | 84 |

Median **0.4 km**, max **41.7 km**. So **78/84 airports sit within 1 km of a road** — snapping the air
endpoint straight onto the nearest road node is well-defined for them. The **6 remaining are bush fields**
(Barter Island, Little Diomede, etc., > 1 km, up to 41.7 km) with no nearby road.

## Snapped variant vs current — same connectivity, cleaner graph
Replacing each air↔road transfer with a **snap** (merge the air node into its nearest road node, drop the
transfer edge):

| network | nodes | edges | components | giant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| current (transfers) | 82,376 | 90,956 | 67 | 98.6 % |
| **snapped (air→road)** | 82,298 | 90,878 | **67** | **98.6 %** |

**Connectivity is identical** (67 components, giant 98.6 %) — snapping changes the *representation*, not the
graph: it removes **78 fabricated `Transfer` edges** and merges the **78 airports onto the road** as shared
nodes, exactly as the requirement asks ("snap, not transfer").

## Recommendation (engine change — follow-up, not done here)
1. **Drop** the `Road↔Plane @ airports` transfer rule.
2. **Snap each air-leg airport endpoint onto its nearest road node** within a cap (~1 km, or the 10 km used
   today) — reuse the hub-snap idiom in `connect_multimodal` phase 2 (`sjoin_nearest` onto the ground), or
   the legacy `assemble.build_multimodal_native` air-translation ("air begins at the airport on the road").
   So the Air leg starts/ends *at* the road node — a shared connection node, no transfer edge.
3. The **6 bush airports** beyond the cap have no road to snap to; they stay connected only through the air
   network (to a hub that is itself on the road) — i.e. air-only, which is correct.

## Where the connection / snap code lives
- Air↔road transfer (current): `mmnet/assemble.py::connect_multimodal` phase 3 + `profile.yaml:156`.
- Snap precedents to reuse: `connect_multimodal` phase 2 (hubs → road∪ice via `sjoin_nearest`);
  `assemble.build_multimodal_native` (air translated onto the road backbone).
