# Connecting road + ice via the waterway — the mmnet framework + connect-to-giant

Connects road + ice through the full Alaska waterway, coherent with mmnet: **anchors = ports ∪ barge hubs**
(Barge↔Road / Barge↔IceRoad transfers); the **before-policies** (road↔road weld, ice↔ice weld, ice↔road
bridge); and a final **connect-to-giant** pass that joins every still-disconnected piece to the giant where
it is physically close. Generated by `02_connect_via_ports.py` (config: AK-only waterway, anchor max_dist
5 km, policies 3 km, connect-to-giant ≤ 2 km).

## The root cause — and the fix (the North Slope)

The big **North Slope (Barrow) cluster (947 nodes)** sat only **210 m** from the green giant yet stayed red,
because: (a) road↔waterway transfers happen **only at ports**, and there is **no Arctic port** in the data;
(b) the welds connect each piece to its **nearest other component** (often another disconnected piece), not
to the giant. So the North Slope welded internally and never reached the green waterway 210 m away.

The **connect-to-giant pass** fixes it: for each still-disconnected ground (road/ice) component, connect its
nearest node to the nearest **giant** node within `CONNECT_MAXD` (2 km). Where the giant side is the
waterway → a **`shore:Barge↔Road/IceRoad`** coastal barge landing (Barrow is sealift-served — physically
real); where it is road/ice → a **`weld:to-giant`** noding gap. **Ice jumps 5 % → 95 %** as the 947-node
Barrow cluster joins; the **North Slope is now connected**. Genuinely remote pieces (gap > 2 km) stay
isolated by design.

## Coherence with mmnet

Faithful to `assemble.connect_multimodal`: ports + barge hubs anchor Barge↔(road∪ice) transfers; the
road↔road / ice↔ice / ice↔road policies apply; the connect-to-giant pass is the principled "weld leftovers"
— joining each piece to the **giant** within a tolerance (a coastal barge landing where road meets sea).
The built pipeline misses all this only because its **waterway layer is facility-bbox-clipped**; the full
AK waterway unlocks it.
