# Alaska air-cargo network — structure + coverage (new Flights data)

Built from the new self-contained `data/raw/connectivity/air/flight_paths_combined.csv` (98 legs, embedded coords +
AK DOT&PF registry IDs). Generated by `01_air_network.py` + `02_structure.py`. Research only.

## The network
- **86 airports, 98 cargo legs** (94 unique OD pairs; a few flown by two carriers). Leg length **9–883 km**
  (median 199 km).
- **2 components**: a giant of **84 airports (98 %)** plus one off-giant pair — **Kenai (ENA) ↔ Nondalton
  (5NN)**, a leg never linked to the trunk.
- Carriers: **Lynden 66 · Everts 28 · Bering Air 4** legs.

## Hub-and-spoke hierarchy
| tier | airports | examples (degree) |
|---|---|---|
| **trunk** | 2 | FAI (19), ANC (10) |
| **regional hub** | 3 | **Bethel (32)**, Nome (19), Kotzebue (13) |
| **relay** | 9 | Emmonak (4), Aniak (3), Unalakleet (3), St Mary's (3), Dillingham/Galena/King Salmon/… (2) |
| **spoke** | 72 | one leg each (Anaktuvuk Pass, Fort Yukon, Point Hope, Shungnak, …) |

Degree is sharply skewed (min 1, median 1, mean 2.2, max 32): a handful of hubs carry the network. **Bethel
is the dominant hub** — the Kuskokwim-delta feeder fan — ahead of the ANC/FAI mainline trunk. The shape is
the classic two-level Alaska bush-cargo system: jet trunk into regional hubs, turboprop/piston feed out to
the villages.

## Attributes
- **Region**: Northern 52 · Central 31 · Southcoast 2 (+1 blank).
- **Owner**: DOT&PF Controlled 78 · Other Government 6 · US Air Force 1 (Tin City).
- **Status**: Standard 53 · SubStandard 25 · Other 7 — i.e. ~29 % of served fields are sub-standard/other
  strips, consistent with remote village runways.

## Coverage of the AK airport registry
- **85 of 285 registry airports (30 %)** carry a cargo leg in this dataset; **200 have none**.
- By region: **Northern 51/118 (43 %)**, **Central 31/80 (39 %)**, **Southcoast 3/87 (3 %)**. The Southeast
  panhandle and the Aleutians are almost absent — those communities move fuel by ferry/barge, not air cargo.
- Northernmost served field: **Kaktovik / Barter Island (BTI)** on the Beaufort coast.

## Takeaways
1. The new data yields a clean, near-fully-connected (98 %) hub-and-spoke air-cargo graph with proper
   coordinates and IDs — no lower-48 geocoding artifacts.
2. **Bethel, not Anchorage, is the structural center** of the cargo feed (Kuskokwim delta).
3. Coverage is **Northern/Western**; the Southeast/Aleutian gap is real (different mode), not a data error —
   except the **Kenai↔Nondalton** off-giant pair, which is a genuine connectivity gap to flag.
4. Route-set differs from the old OD list (no Ryan Air delta spokes here) — reconciliation is future work
   (see `DATA_COMPARISON.md`).
