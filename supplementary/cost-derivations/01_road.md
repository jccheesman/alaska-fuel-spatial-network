# Road (highway tanker) — $/gallon-mile derivation

**Tooling note (required disclosure):** This chapter was originally drafted from **WebSearch result snippets** because `WebFetch` was permission-blocked. On **2026-07-15** the two primary state PDFs (OPPM heating-oil contract; DEC Fairbanks ULS/HS cost analysis) were fetched and read page-by-page, and the load-bearing numbers were verified or corrected in place (see the first Verification addendum). On **2026-07-17** a **second pass** was run against locally downloaded primary documents: the full **ATRI 2025 report PDF** (previously gated), the **Econ One** source deck behind the DEC transport figures, and the state **marine-diesel contract**. The ATRI headline and line-item figures are now verified against the primary tables; the Econ One transport figures' mode assignments were read directly off the source map; and the marine contract was found to contribute no road-system points (see the second Verification addendum at the end).

## 1. Search strategy

Three independent evidence tracks were pursued in parallel, mirroring the three required methods:

1. **Tariff / contract track** — queries for Alaska fuel-hauling rates in ¢/gallon, State of Alaska bulk-fuel procurement documents (Office of Procurement & Property Management statewide contracts, Online Public Notices bid tabulations), and regulatory cost filings (Alaska DEC ULSD transition cost analyses, which contain carrier-quoted route transport costs).
2. **Cost build-up track** — queries for ATRI *Operational Costs of Trucking* (2025 update, 2024 data), sector-level (specialized/tanker) $/mile, Alaska-specific trucking rates and driver-wage premiums, and Alaska highway tanker payload capacities (Colville, Petro Star/Sourdough, Dalton Highway operations).
3. **Revealed-price track** — queries for the DCRA Alaska Fuel Price Survey (Winter 2026 report), AHFC fuel survey, ISER fuel-cost-components studies, and per-community heating-oil prices along the road system (Glennallen, Delta Junction, Tok, Valdez, Fairbanks).

Government PDFs and regulatory filings were prioritized over trade-press and broker marketing pages. Inflation adjustment uses BLS CPI-U.

## 3. Method A / B / C — full derivations with arithmetic

### Method A — Regulatory filing: bulk line-haul cost, Valdez refinery → Fairbanks (DEC report, citing Econ One)

Verified against the DEC PDF, p. 15 (§1.3): the 20 ¢ and 16 ¢ figures appear verbatim, attributed to Econ One. The Econ One source deck (Pulliam/AOGA, December 2016) was read on the second pass; its **map on p. 15 ("Cost of In-State Product Movement Can Be Significant")** codes the **Valdez→Fairbanks 20 ¢/gal movement as TRUCK (green)** and Anchorage→Fairbanks 16 ¢/gal as RAIL (red) via the legend — so the mode is now confirmed directly, not merely by geographic necessity. Vintage: DEC cites a 2015 Econ One report; the 2016 deck carries no explicit dollar-year, so the **2015→2026 CPI factor (1.409)** established on the first pass is retained (a 2016 basis would give ~1.375, immaterial at this rounding).

- Transport cost, Valdez refinery → Fairbanks (**truck, confirmed by Econ One's mode legend**): **$0.20/gal** over **364 mi** (2015$ basis).
- $0.20 ÷ 364 mi = **$0.000549/gal-mi (2015$)**
- 2026$: 0.000549 × 1.409 = **$0.000774/gal-mi ≈ $0.00077/gal-mi**
- Cross-modal sanity check: rail Anchorage→Fairbanks $0.16 ÷ 356 mi = $0.000449 (2015$) → $0.000633 (2026$). Rail correctly comes in below truck; truck/rail per-mile ratio 1.22 is plausible. (The Econ One map also shows a separate ≈ 2 ¢/gal **pipeline** Kenai→Anchorage and a ≈ 10 ¢/gal **barge** leg — neither is a truck movement, so both are excluded from Method A.)
- This is a refinery-to-rack bulk movement in ~10,000–13,000-gal units — the closest published analogue to a routing-model edge cost. Includes carrier operating cost + margin; excludes rack fees and distributor margin.

### Method B — Cost build-up: Alaska tanker $/mile ÷ payload

- National anchor (**ATRI 2024, verified primary tables**): Specialized sector **$2.32/truck-mile** (Table 11, p. 17); industry average **$2.260/mi** (Table 8, p. 15). Tank operations fall inside the Specialized sector — ATRI publishes no separate tanker line-item — so $2.32 is the correct national starting point.
- Alaska uplift: driver comp ≈ 1.8–2× national (wages **$0.798** + benefits **$0.197** = **$0.995/mi** of the ATRI 2024 total, Table 8 → +$0.80–1.00/mi at 1.8–2×); Alaska diesel above national average (fuel **$0.481/mi** → +~$0.08/mi); harsher R&M ($0.198/mi base). Implied Alaska tanker cost ≈ $2.95–$3.25/mi. Independent market evidence: Alaska freight rates **$2.80–$3.10/mi** (rate basis, margin included); a broker page verified by fetch (heavyequipmenttransport.com) quotes Alaska truck services at **$3.50–$6.00/mi**, but that is heavy-equipment/oversize-oriented and skews high — treated as corroboration that Alaska rates sit well above the national $2.26 average, not as a tanker rate. **Adopt $3.00/truck-mile (2025–26$, margin included).**
- Payload: **10,500 gal** central (Dalton semis ~10,000 gal; Petro Star A-trains 13,000 gal; conservative single-trailer 9,000 gal).
- Deadhead: fuel tankers return empty → round-trip factor **1.9** applied to the one-way delivery distance.
- Central: ($3.00 × 1.9) ÷ 10,500 = 5.70 ÷ 10,500 = **$0.000543/gal-mi (≈2026$)**
- Low: ($2.80 × 1.8) ÷ 13,000 = 5.04 ÷ 13,000 = **$0.000388**
- High: ($3.25 × 2.0) ÷ 9,000 = 6.50 ÷ 9,000 = **$0.000722**
- **Method B: $0.00054/gal-mi (range $0.00039–$0.00072), 2026$.**

**Method C: $0.00067/gal-mi central (11-point OLS, 2026$); specification band $0.00044–$0.00083; bulk-comparable cross-check $0.00060.** Biased high relative to pure line-haul because contract prices are delivered-to-tank (include final-mile service).

## 5. Cross-method comparison and reconciliation

| Method | Basis | 2026 $/gal-mi | Bias assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — DEC regulatory line-haul (Valdez→Fairbanks) | Bulk refinery→rack **truck** movement (mode confirmed via Econ One map), carrier-quoted | **0.00077** (0.00055 in 2015$) | Most directly on-target for an edge cost; single route, 2015 vintage inflated by CPI (trucking costs rose faster than CPI 2015–24, so possibly slightly low) |
| B — Cost build-up (ATRI + AK uplift ÷ payload) | Operating cost/market rate × deadhead ÷ gallons | **0.00054** (0.00039–0.00072) | Sensitive to payload and deadhead assumptions; margin included via market-rate basis; **ATRI inputs now verified against the primary tables** (Specialized $2.32/mi, line-items per Table 8), so the national anchor is firm — residual uncertainty is the Alaska uplift and payload, not the base data |
| C — State contract fee gradient (11-point, FY27) | Delivered-price vs distance, within vendor/award | **0.00067** OLS (specification band 0.00044–0.00083); Fairbanks–Delta cross-check **0.00060** | Includes final-mile small-drop service, not just line-haul; much better constrained after verification (11 points, R² ≈ 0.75) |

The three methods bracket a band of roughly **$0.0004–$0.0011 per gallon-mile**, with the three central estimates (A = 0.00077, B = 0.00054, C = 0.00067) and the bulk-comparable cross-check (0.00060) clustering tightly around **$0.0006–$0.0008**. Verification *tightened* the cluster: A rose slightly (2015 vintage correction), and C's expanded regression fell from the knowingly-high 3-point value (0.00083) toward the middle of the band. Method B's low end assumes best-case 13,000-gal A-trains with efficient backhaul. The contract intercept (≈$0.20–0.27/gal fixed, depending on specification) and the Two Rivers outlier ($0.87/gal FY27 at ~25 mi) confirm that *fixed* per-delivery costs dominate short hauls — reinforcing that the per-mile slope, not the total differential, is the right routing-model quantity.
