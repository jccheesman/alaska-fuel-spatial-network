# 05 — Intermodal Transfer Fees

**Tooling / provenance note (required disclosure):** This chapter synthesizes a blind three-method derivation (tariff build-up / operational build-up / revealed-cost decomposition) for each of seven modal-handoff fees, each run past two independent adversarial skeptic panels and a local-document cross-check. Every load-bearing figure was verified verbatim against its primary source (Crowley Bethel rate sheet; Port of Bethel Tariff #5 with 2026 rate columns; Port of Nome Tariff 16.4/16.5; Port of Alaska Tariff 10.1/10.2; ISER 2010 *Components of Alaska Fuel Costs*; ATRI 2025 Update; AK DOL Pamphlet 600 Issue 52; AEA FY2025 PCE report; NSB FY26-27 budget book; NWAB Noatak Winter Fuel Haul System). All values are **2026 dollars**. Where a method was refuted by the skeptics it is discounted below; where methods diverged, the value with the strongest surviving provenance is chosen and the divergence is explained.

**Architecture (fixed, not re-litigated here):** the blended **Barge** per-gallon-mile rate (0.0010) already absorbs lightering + most terminal handling (revealed at $0.8-1.2/gal on Bethel-type routes), so the fee table carries **no lightering entry**. The **Road** rate (0.0007) and **IceRoad** rate (0.010) are carriage-only, so rack/handling/receiving fees legitimately live in the fee table. Every derivation below excludes carriage, storage rent, product cost, and taxes by construction.

## 05.1 — ("barge","storage"): barge discharge into hub tank farm (marine header fee)

**Event:** one line-haul barge discharging diesel/heating oil across a marine header into a western-Alaska hub tank farm (Bethel/Nome/Kotzebue class). Excludes carriage, lightering, storage rental, truck rack.

**Tariff — $0.17 (range 0.10-0.23).** Two builds bracketing the event. Municipal wharfage: Port of Bethel Tariff #5 §206(D) inbound petroleum $0.06/gal (2026 column); Port of Nome 16.5 $0.048/gal (2025) → $0.049 (2026$). Terminal-operator header fee: Crowley Bethel rate sheet Marine Header $0.1500/gal (eff. 8/10/2022) × CPI 1.11 → $0.167. Cost-side floor = wharfage $0.048-0.06 + ISER-2010 shore-side offload labor $0.04-0.06 × 1.51 = $0.06-0.09 + dockage ~$0.001 → **$0.11-0.15**. Market build = Crowley $0.167 + wharfage where a city dock is crossed → **$0.167-0.227**. Point = CPI-adjusted Crowley header ($0.17).

**Ops — $0.09 (range 0.06-0.15).** 2.5M-gal event, 1,500 gpm effective, ~31.8 h. Bottom-up shore-side ops (crew + monitoring + header amortization + spill readiness) ≈ $0.032/gal + municipal wharfage $0.055 = $0.086. ISER anchors the high end ($0.15) via fully-allocated 24-h receiving labor.

**Revealed — $0.15 (range 0.09-0.22).** ISER cost-build (a) offload labor $0.04-0.06 + (b) city dock fee $0.02-0.04 (2010$), terminal-space fee $0.07-0.15 correctly excluded as storage. (a)+(b) × 1.51 = $0.09-0.15. Bethel/Nome tariff cross-checks $0.11-0.21. Point $0.15.

**Skeptics.** All three methods **upheld** on source integrity — every figure located verbatim, CPI arithmetic correct within ~2%. Two corrections shared by both panels: (1) the ops **$0.016/gal vessel-time-on-the-clock is a double-count** (port/discharge time lives in the blended barge per-mile rate) → drop it, but ops simultaneously **under-counts allocated shore labor** vs ISER's fully-loaded $0.06-0.09, netting to ~$0.08-0.11; (2) the revealed derivation **misreads ISER's $0.10-0.15 "missing-header" differential** — that is a carriage-difficulty premium for header-less communities, not a discharge fee — and its additive Bethel double-stack is probably a single levy recorded twice → drop both → ~$0.12. Panel 1 recommended **$0.13 (0.09-0.18)**; Panel 2 recommended **$0.11 (0.07-0.17)**.

**Local cross-check.** ISER is the only on-point local PDF; all cited figures verbatim. The city-dock fee ($0.02-0.04, 2010$) CPI-adjusts almost exactly onto today's Nome/Bethel wharfage — independent validation of both the inflation method and the tariff floor. Local reading **confirms** the skeptic corrections (missing-header differential is a transport-charge variation, not a discharge fee) and endorses **~$0.11, range $0.07-0.17**.

**Convergence & final.** The Crowley $0.167 is a genuine market price but is the *third-party-throughput ceiling*; most hub tank farms are carrier-owned (Crowley Bethel/Kotzebue, Bonanza Nome) and pay the internal cost, not the posted margin. The verified cost build (municipal wharfage $0.05-0.06 + fully-allocated shore-side receiving $0.06-0.09) is $0.11-0.15. Weighting the two skeptic panels (0.13, 0.11) and the local ISER-anchored $0.11:

> **Final: $0.12/gal, range $0.07-0.17 (2026$), medium confidence.**

## 05.2 — ("storage","overland"): tank-farm loading rack onto tanker truck (truck rack fee)

**Event:** one truck-rack loading event at a hub bulk terminal (rack use, metering, loading labor, VCU). Excludes storage rental, marine intake, carriage.

**Tariff — $0.25 (range 0.01-0.30).** Exact-match anchor: Crowley Bethel Table 2 Truck Rack $0.2500/gal (eff. 8/10/2022), rack-only by construction (storage $2.25/bbl-mo and marine header $0.15 are separate line items). CPI × 1.114 → $0.279 (2026$). ISER third-party terminal fee $0.07-0.15 (2010$) × 1.51 = $0.11-0.23 brackets just below. Urban low-end (Valero MTSA, AIAS flowage) ~$0.01. Rural-hub-dominated network → point $0.25, shaded just below the CPI-adjusted Crowley figure.

**Ops — $0.12 (range 0.02-0.28).** 50/50 urban/rural blend of an engineering build-up (rural bottom-up $0.13 vs Crowley cross-check $0.28 → rural rep ~$0.20; urban ~$0.03).

**Revealed — $0.21 (range 0.05-0.32).** Crowley $0.28 (rural) 2/3-weighted with a state-heating-oil-contract urban intercept (~$0.06) 1/3-weighted → $0.207.

**Skeptics.** Both panels **upheld the tariff method** (Crowley figure verbatim-verified, storage separately billed → genuinely rack-only, no carriage double-count) and **refuted ops and revealed**: ops' $0.12 rests on unsourced rack-capital/throughput assumptions and a 50/50 blend whose urban leg is fabricated (no observed Alaska road-system rack fee); revealed's urban intercept is an unfounded decomposition of a bundled state-contract delivered price plus a mis-scoped airport *flowage tax*. Corrected toward the sole real anchor, both collapse to ~$0.20-0.25. Shared dollar-year nit: actual June-2026 CPI-U is 327.075 (not 330-334), so CPI-correct Crowley is $0.276 not $0.282 (immaterial). Panel 1 recommended **$0.22 (0.03-0.30)**; Panel 2 recommended **$0.24 (0.10-0.30)**.

**Local cross-check.** ISER has no truck-rack-specific line — only terminal-*access* ($0.07-0.15) and terminal-*use* ($0.03), both broader than a pure rack event. CPI-corrected (× 1.500) that is $0.105-0.225 and $0.045. The derived point sits at/above the top of the access band, defensible *only* because it rests on the external Crowley rack tariff. Local recommends **modest downward shading to ~$0.20**, range low to ~$0.03-0.045, and strongly endorses bimodality (~$0.03-0.06 road-system racks vs ~$0.20-0.25 remote barge hubs).

**Convergence & final.** One verified exact-match Alaska tariff (Crowley $0.276 CPI-2026$) anchors everything; the debate is only how far to shade for embedded sole-provider rent (ISER's cost-structure "terminal use" is ~8× lower). Panels landed 0.22-0.24; local ISER pulls to ~0.20. Splitting:

> **Final: $0.22/gal, range $0.05-0.30 (2026$), medium confidence.** Strongly bimodal — if the model can key on hub class, use ~$0.05 for road-system racks and ~$0.25 for remote Western-Alaska barge hubs rather than one blended value.

## 05.3 — ("barge","overland"): direct barge-to-truck handoff at a landing

Two readings must be kept separate, because the model uses this key two ways.

**(a) Component-sum through hub storage** (the model's default construction). This key = barge→storage + storage→overland. With the retuned legs, **$0.12 + $0.22 = $0.34/gal (range 0.12-0.47)**.

**(b) Genuine direct barge-to-truck at a landing** (no tank transit). A dedicated derivation priced this event:

- **Tariff — $0.12 (0.05-0.22):** landing/dock fee (Port of Alaska Item 260 fuel-transfer 1.90¢/gal 2026; Bethel §200.E $0.06/gal; ISER city-dock $0.03-0.06) ~$0.05 + shore-side offload labor (ISER $0.04-0.06 × 1.5 = $0.06-0.09) ~$0.07.
- **Ops — $0.16 (0.07-0.40)** and **Revealed — $0.22 (0.12-0.40):** both dominated by barge-standby / "no-terminal slow-discharge premium" components.

**Skeptics.** Both panels **refuted ops and revealed** and **upheld only the tariff build**. The decisive fault is scope: ops re-charges ~2/3 of its number as barge standby-hours, and revealed rebuilds ISER's missing-header ($0.10-0.15) and deficiency (+$0.20) *delivered-price variations* — both are barge carriage/dwell already smeared into the 0.0010 barge per-mile rate. Panel 1 (stripping only the double-count) converged **$0.12 (0.06-0.20)**; Panel 2 (also noting the model already charges $0.40 here and the barge rate carries offload labor) converged **$0.08 (0.04-0.14)**.

**Local cross-check.** ISER substantiates both critiques verbatim: the $0.10-0.15 figure is a transportation-charge variation (p.23), the barge's own carriage is fully allocated at $0.45/gal (Table 1), and Table 4 assigns only $0.03 to discrete "terminal use." Local recommends **~$0.10, range $0.05-0.15** for the direct event, explicitly *not* carrying the $0.22-0.40 highs.

**Convergence & final.** For the **component-sum construction the model actually uses**, the honest value is the sum of the two retuned legs:

> **Final (component path): $0.34/gal, range $0.12-0.47 (2026$), medium confidence.**
> **If this key ever fires on a true direct landing (no storage): use ~$0.10/gal, range $0.06-0.20** (surviving tariff build; ops/revealed refuted for double-counting barge dwell).

## 05.4 — ("plane","overland"): air-cargo fuel offload / tarmac pumping

**Event:** ~40-min engines-off tarmac offload of a DC-6/C-46 bulk-fuel load (2,000-4,000 gal, 3,000 divisor) into a truck or village tanks. Excludes air carriage, landing fees, avgas.

**Tariff — $0.12 (0.05-0.25).** No standalone tarmac tariff exists; Everts sells charter-bundled. Build: 3-person crew self-offload (Everts crews load/unload their own aircraft) ~$463/hr × 0.65 h ÷ 3,000 = $0.100; onboard pump/hose amortization $0.012; representative AIAS non-aviation flowage $0.01 → $0.122.

**Ops — $0.15 (0.04-0.42).** Same crew + truck standby + **$350/hr aircraft ground-standby** ($262.50, 59% of the mid case) + equipment → $0.149.

**Revealed — $0.13 (0.06-0.25).** Heating-oil-contract intercept allocation + bottom-up ($0.105) + marine analogues, plus a probability-weighted **$0.02 flowage adder**.

**Skeptics.** Both panels **upheld tariff** (least double-count-exposed — omits airframe standby) and **refuted ops and revealed**. Ops' $350/hr aircraft ground-standby is already inside the all-in air per-gal-mile rate, and the cited charter contract proves the first 2 h of load/unload standby are *free/bundled* → strip it → ops ~$0.06. Revealed's flowage adder mis-applies the DOT&PF fee (it is on airport fuel *sales*/dispensing, heating fuel *exempt*, not a cargo transfer) → strip it → revealed ~$0.10; its marine terminal analogues are the wrong physical event. Both panels recommended **~$0.09 (0.05-0.16)**.

**Local cross-check.** ISER contains **no air-tarmac figure at all**; it is entirely marine/barge + trucking. Critical provenance defect flagged: the "$1.25/gal per 100 air-miles / DC-6 4,900 gal" cross-check attributed to this local PDF **is not in it** (it belongs to a separate 2008 ISER report). The one comparable datum — shore-side offload *labor* $0.04-0.06/gal (p.18) — sits at the low-mid of the derived range and loosely corroborates it. Local: keep **~$0.09, range $0.05-0.16**; remove the mis-cited air-carriage anchor.

**Convergence & final.** The double-count-clean labor+equipment core is $0.06-0.11 across all three; add-ons (airframe standby, flowage) were refuted.

> **Final: $0.10/gal, range $0.05-0.16 (2026$), low confidence** (~85% engineering build-up; no published tarmac tariff). If the air per-gal-mile rate is confirmed built from all-in charter pricing, the marginal billed offload trends to $0.05-0.06.

## 05.5 — ("plane","drums"): manual offload of drummed fuel from aircraft

**Event:** manual offload of 55-gal fuel drums from a small aircraft at a village strip. Scope explicitly **excludes** the drum packaging as a commodity input (retail layer) and includes only handling-cycle drum attrition.

**Tariff — $0.85 (0.45-1.50).** Labor (Pamphlet 600 A0710 Material Handler $52.66/hr, 1.25 person-h ÷ 165 gal) $0.40 + equipment $0.04 + **handling-cycle drum attrition** (10%/cycle × ~$130 delivered) $0.24 + spill/hazmat $0.18 = $0.86.

**Ops — $0.80 (0.45-2.00).** Same structure but drum term = **full new-drum capital** ($110 ÷ 4 fills = $150/event = $0.545/gal, 66% of the point).

**Revealed — $0.60 (0.35-1.10).** PCE decomposition: Takotna $5.63 vs supply hub McGrath $4.96 → $0.68 gap; net air carriage & origin load-out → ~$0.33 destination residual + drum amortization → ~$0.58-0.65.

**Skeptics.** The scope-decisive issue is whether new-drum capital is in-scope. **Both panels refuted ops** (its dominant term is full drum capital, which the model's separate commodity/retail layer carries; its own caveat concedes the correction to $0.25-0.30). **Tariff and revealed upheld** — both scope the drum term to attrition/loss. The two panels then diverge on the residual: **Panel 1** (attrition-*included* as legitimate handling damage) → **$0.62 (0.40-1.10)**; **Panel 2** (strip *all* drum capital, keep only offload-cycle damage) → **$0.35 (0.20-0.65)**.

**Local cross-check.** ISER has no drum figure; its closest analog (marine 24-h linehaul offload labor $0.04-0.06/gal over 250,000+ gal) is 10-20× lower purely because volume-per-event is enormous — which confirms the *premise* (per-gallon handling explodes at 110-275-gal loads) without independently pinning magnitude. Its spill/admin benchmark ($0.05-0.10/gal, volume-spread) modestly favors the stricter Panel 2's lean spill line over the tariff's $0.18. Local leans toward **~$0.35 (0.20-0.65)** but calls it a lean, not a mandate.

**Convergence & final.** The two scope-clean skeptic centers straddle $0.35 (drum-capital-excluded) and $0.62 (attrition-included). The current $0.50 placeholder sits squarely between them and inside both ranges; with real provenance now behind it, it is a defensible single value pending a model decision on the drum-commodity layer.

> **Final: $0.50/gal, range $0.20-1.10 (2026$), low confidence.** Conditional guidance: if the model prices drums in a separate commodity layer, use **~$0.35**; if this fee is meant to carry drum replacement, use **~$0.62**. Per-gallon cost scales inversely with shipment size (single-drum C207 loads push toward the high end).

## 05.6 — ("overland","ice_road"): highway tanker to ice-road truck via hub storage

**Event:** offload a highway tanker into Dalton/Deadhorse hub storage + reload a ~3,000-gal ice-road tanker (two passes). Excludes highway and ice-road carriage.

**Tariff — $0.16 (0.10-0.25).** ISER item-3 third-party terminal (through-)fee $0.07-0.15 (2010$) × 1.50 = $0.105-0.225, midpoint $0.165; small-lot reload uplift and truck-cheaper-than-marine-intake offsets net ~0. POA Item 260 1.90¢/gal and Nome wharfage bracket.

**Ops — $0.10 (0.05-0.20).** Bottom-up: Pamphlet 600 A2101 (incl. "Fueler/Oil Distributor Truck") $84.34/hr fully-loaded + ATRI standby $20.17/hr + pump rates → Step A (8,500-gal offload) $0.035 + Step B (3,000-gal reload) $0.067 = $0.102.

**Revealed — $0.30 (0.16-0.45).** Anchors on Crowley Bethel in+out ($0.15 marine header + $0.25 rack) × CPI → $0.28-0.45, picking $0.30 on a "sole-provider high-cost like Bethel" premium.

**Skeptics.** Both panels **refuted revealed**: its intake leg is a **marine header** applied to a **highway-tanker** intake (double-prices offload the road rate already covers), and its monopoly premium is wrong for a road-connected hub with multiple operators (Colville, Crowley, Inlet) — its own bottom-up cross-check lands $0.14-0.19. **Tariff upheld** but leans slightly high (stacks a small-lot uplift on a range that already prices full terminal use) → ~$0.14. **Ops upheld** as the cleanest build (best-sourced labor) but is a marginal-*cost* floor missing O&M/capital/margin → grossed up ~$0.14. Panel 1 recommended **$0.12 (0.08-0.20)**; Panel 2 recommended **$0.17 (0.10-0.30)**.

**Local cross-check.** NSB FY26-27 budget confirms a real in-house Deadhorse/North-Slope hub operator (FNG division, NSB-owned tank farms, Deadhorse HBTC) — supporting the *cost-basis* framing over the monopoly-margin marine tariff, i.e. favoring the downward correction of revealed. Noatak PDF confirms the store-then-reload chain and 3,000-5,000-gal parcel. No per-gallon rate in any local doc, so no numeric move.

**Convergence & final.** Corrected estimates converge $0.09 (ops) / $0.14 (tariff) / $0.18 (revealed-adjusted); panels centered 0.12-0.17.

> **Final: $0.14/gal, range $0.08-0.25 (2026$), medium confidence.** High end accommodates a genuine Deadhorse camp/per-diem premium + small-lot reload; low end removes any hub-O&M carried elsewhere in the model.

## 05.7 — ("ice_road","storage"): ice-road truck discharge into village tank farm

**Event:** single discharge of an ice-road tanker/cat-train (3,000-5,000 gal) into a village tank farm — one pass. Excludes ice-road carriage, tank-farm O&M.

**Tariff — $0.22 (0.10-0.35).** Nome wharfage $0.049 + 2-person × 2.0-h receiving labor (Nome $115/$180 blended $156/hr) $0.156 + equipment $0.011 = $0.216. Crowley rack $0.28 as upper anchor.

**Ops — $0.07 (0.04-0.16).** Bottom-up: 1.75-h event, cold-derated pump, driver-operator + village operator + ATRI equipment standing → $279/event ÷ 4,000 gal = $0.070.

**Revealed — $0.15 (0.08-0.25).** PCE: Atqasuk $7.41 vs four barge-direct NSB comparators mean $5.71 → $1.70 winter-haul premium; net terminal + carriage, time-proportional discharge share (8.2%) → $0.111 + village receiving $0.034 = $0.144.

**Skeptics.** Both panels **refuted the tariff method**: its anchors (Crowley $0.25 rack, Nome wharfage) are the wrong unit of service — a monopoly terminal-throughput fee + a marine wharfage applied by assumed symmetry to a truck event — and ISER Table-4 "terminal use" ($0.03/gal) makes a $0.22 bare-discharge handoff implausibly large; its 2-person × 2.0-h labor is the unsupported 71%-of-total driver. **Ops upheld** ($0.07, verified inputs). **Revealed upheld** but biased high by un-stripped storage/admin → nudge to ~$0.10. Both panels stress the **delivering crew/rig time is already inside the 0.010 ice-road carriage rate** (which per `blind_derivations/04_ice_road.md` includes a +20% load/unload adder over the full round-trip cycle); **only village-side receiving labor is additive.** Panel 1 recommended **$0.08 (0.05-0.15)**; Panel 2 (pure carriage-scope lens) recommended **$0.05 (0.03-0.09)**.

**Local cross-check.** No local doc prices the discharge event. Two citation corrections: NWFHLS capital is **$425,000** (not the $350,000 both ops/revealed cited — a $75k sub-line error, immaterial to their soft cross-check); and the "200 trips / 350-400k gal" figures the second skeptic panel called fabricated **are in fact present on Noatak p.10** — that skeptic point is itself inaccurate. NSB village Fuel cost centers (Atqasuk 6108.FUEL $472,913; Nuiqsut 6110.FUEL $569,184) are in-house staffing with no per-gallon line, supporting the ops cost-basis framing. No numeric move.

**Convergence & final.** Ops ($0.07) and revealed ($0.10, biased high) converge once the tariff outlier is corrected down; the binding architectural fact is that delivering-side handling is already in the ice-road per-mile rate, leaving only village receiving.

> **Final: $0.08/gal, range $0.04-0.15 (2026$), medium confidence.** Dominant sensitivities: load size (3,000 vs 5,000 gal) and whether a dedicated village receiving operator is required (driver self-discharge → floor).

## 05.8 — ("barge","ice_road"): never-traversed placeholder edge

**Status (verified 2026-07-23):** a same-month barge↔ice-road handoff is temporally impossible (ice roads Jan-Mar, barges May-Oct; zero month-overlap at all 8 North Slope sites), so monthly Dijkstra routing **never traverses this edge**. It exists only to satisfy the no-costless-edge rule. The real flow is inter-temporal through hub storage: barge→storage in summer, storage→ice-road in winter.

**Composition.** Mirror the two true component legs with their retuned values: barge→storage **$0.12** (summer intake) + a storage-side ice-road **loading** leg (the winter half of the store-then-reload chain, ~$0.08-0.14 per §05.7's one-pass framing) → component sum **~$0.20**.

> **Final: $0.20/gal, range $0.11-0.32 (2026$), low confidence.** Because the edge is never exercised, the exact value is immaterial to results; it is set to the honest component sum rather than the stale $0.40. Keep the `marine_header_fee` + loading sub-key structure so a future inventory-dynamics phase (Phase 6) can decompose it into the two temporal legs.

## Carry-forward limitations (values still resting on placeholder- or thin-grade evidence)

1. **("plane","drums") — scope-unresolved (low).** The point value depends on a model decision not yet made: whether new-drum capital lives in a separate commodity/retail layer (→ ~$0.35) or is folded into this fee (→ ~$0.62). $0.50 is a compromise. The two dominant quantities (drum fills-before-loss; per-gallon handling time on 110-275-gal loads) have **no documented Alaska value**. Resolve by (a) fixing the drum-commodity-layer convention in the model and (b) obtaining an Everts/Ryan drum-handling or deposit figure.

2. **("plane","overland") — ~85% engineering build-up (low).** No carrier or airport publishes a standalone tarmac fuel-offload tariff; the point rests on crew-time and pump-rate estimates. **Provenance fix required:** the ISER "$1.25/gal/100mi air-carriage" cross-check is mis-cited to the local ISER 2010 PDF — that figure is not in that document (belongs to the separate 2008 ISER *Components of Delivered Fuel Prices* report). Re-attribute or drop. Firm up with an awarded bid schedule under state Air Cargo contract 240000071 (Everts Additional Standby Time $/hr).

3. **("ice_road","storage") and ("overland","ice_road") — no published Deadhorse/North-Slope tariff (medium).** North Slope hub terminals (Colville, Brice, Crowley Deadhorse) are private and publish no throughput fees; both values are bottom-up builds transposed from ISER marine-hub and Bethel commercial rates, cross-checked against Pamphlet 600 labor and ATRI equipment. A single confirmed operator invoice for either handoff would collapse the range. The ice_road→storage value also depends on the still-implicit assumption (from `04_ice_road.md`) that the 0.010 per-mile rate fully absorbs delivering-side discharge dwell — if that rate is ever recalibrated without the load/unload adder, this fee must rise to re-capture delivering-side time.

4. **("barge","ice_road") — never exercised (low, but immaterial).** Correct only up to the accuracy of its two mirrored legs; harmless because Dijkstra cannot traverse it. Revisit only when Phase 6 inventory dynamics make the inter-temporal barge→storage→ice_road path explicit.

5. **All values — CPI to mid-2026 (minor).** Several derivations used a projected mid-2026 CPI-U of ~326-334; the actual June-2026 index is 327.075. The discrepancy moves any point estimate by <$0.006/gal and is below the rounding of every final value here. ISER 2010 figures (the backbone of the marine-hub and receiving-labor anchors) are 16 years old; CPI-U plausibly *understates* rural-Alaska labor/insurance/spill-compliance escalation, biasing the ISER-anchored floors slightly low.

6. **Bimodality not yet keyed (("storage","overland")).** The single blended $0.22 mis-prices both ends of a genuinely bimodal fee (~$0.05 road-system racks vs ~$0.25 remote barge hubs). If the network can distinguish hub class, split this fee rather than carry one value.
